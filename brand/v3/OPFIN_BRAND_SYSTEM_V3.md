# OpFin Brand System v3

Status: Release candidate  
Version: 3.0.0-rc.1  
Updated: 24 September 2026  
Language: English (United Kingdom)

## 1. Brand idea

**OpFin helps people understand their financial position and take the next useful step with clarity.**

Brand promise: **Your next step, clearer.**

Customer proposition: **Understand your money. Make better next moves.**

Platform explanation: OpFin connects everyday money, financial planning, responsible credit and provider-enabled saving, protection and growth in one governed financial platform.

The brand must feel calm, credible, practical, inclusive and progressive. It must not feel speculative, aggressive, exclusive, childish or like a short-term-loan advertisement.

## 2. Identity

### 2.1 Monogram

The OpFin monogram is the primary recognition device. Use the v3 vector master in `assets/opfin-symbol-master.svg`.

Do not redraw, stretch, rotate, add shadows, add gradients, place it inside an arbitrary badge, alter its internal geometry or combine it with another symbol.

### 2.2 Wordmark and lock-ups

Controlled files:

- `assets/opfin-wordmark.svg`
- `assets/opfin-lockup-horizontal.svg`
- `assets/opfin-lockup-stacked.svg`
- `assets/opfin-app-icon-master.svg`

The symbol-only mark is preferred for compact interface, favicon and app-icon use. The horizontal lock-up is preferred in headers and partner materials. The stacked lock-up is reserved for centred covers, controlled campaign artwork and situations with generous vertical space.

### 2.3 Clear space

Let **x** equal one quarter of the monogram width. Maintain at least **1x** clear space on every side of the symbol or lock-up. For partner co-branding use **2x** between the OpFin lock-up, divider and partner mark.

No text, edge, photograph focal point or other logo may enter the clear-space area.

### 2.4 Minimum size

Digital:
- symbol: 24 px minimum;
- horizontal lock-up: 120 px minimum width;
- stacked lock-up: 96 px minimum width.

Print:
- symbol: 8 mm minimum width;
- horizontal lock-up: 32 mm minimum width;
- stacked lock-up: 28 mm minimum width.

When the wordmark becomes hard to read, use the symbol rather than shrinking the lock-up into decorative dust.

## 3. Colour

### Core

| Role | Name | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Indigo | `#353B78` |
| Primary strong | Strong Indigo | `#29305F` |
| Human accent | Apricot | `#F2B38D` |
| Warm background | Warm Ivory | `#FAF8F2` |
| Supporting surface | Periwinkle | `#E8EAF6` |
| Primary text | Ink | `#202436` |
| Secondary text | Muted | `#555D72` |
| Dividers | Line | `#D7DAE8` |
| Canvas | White | `#FFFFFF` |

Semantic colours remain Success `#116B4E`, Warning `#865210` and Danger `#AA2636`. They communicate state, not decoration.

### Approved text combinations

Normal text:
- Ink on White or Warm Ivory;
- Muted on White or Warm Ivory;
- White on Indigo or Strong Indigo;
- Ink on Apricot;
- Strong Indigo on Apricot.

Apricot is an accent, not a body-text colour on a light background. Periwinkle is a supporting surface, not low-contrast copy.

Representative contrast checks in the v3 palette exceed the WCAG 4.5:1 normal-text threshold for the required pairs. Automated checks remain necessary because a palette does not make every possible composition accessible by divine intervention.

## 4. Typography

Primary typeface: **Inter**.  
Fallback: **Arial**, then system sans-serif.

Principles:
- body copy: 16 px / 1.5 minimum on customer Web surfaces where practical;
- labels: 14 px minimum;
- financial numbers: use tabular numerals;
- headings: compact negative tracking only at larger sizes;
- avoid all-capitals for paragraphs;
- uppercase may be used sparingly for short eyebrow labels.

Do not introduce another corporate sans-serif because a campaign designer became restless.

## 5. Layout and shape

- Use a calm 8 px spacing rhythm, with 4 px only for tight internal relationships.
- Customer controls target at least 48 × 48 px.
- Primary cards use 12 px corner radii.
- Large campaign/product-stage surfaces may use 24 px.
- Prefer thin Line borders and very restrained Indigo-tinted shadow.
- Use whitespace to establish hierarchy. Do not fill empty space merely because it exists.

## 6. Proprietary product visual language

### 6.1 Progress Path

The **Progress Path** is the recurring OpFin visual motif. It is derived from the monogram's rounded directional geometry and uses Periwinkle for context with Apricot for the active/next route.

Use it:
- as a quiet background device;
- to connect stages in a journey;
- to underline or point toward the next action;
- in covers, store graphics and campaign framing.

Never run the Progress Path through important text. The Play graphic must underline or guide **clearer.**, never strike it out.

### 6.2 Financial Compass

The Financial Compass is the product's structured view of financial position. It separates:
- recorded facts;
- estimates;
- unavailable information;
- upcoming commitments;
- safe-to-spend or equivalent derived guidance where supported;
- goals and progress.

Visual rules:
- White/Ivory cards with Indigo hierarchy;
- financial figures are prominent but never isolated from provenance or explanation;
- confidence/availability is stated in words, not hidden behind colour;
- use consistent cards rather than gauges that imply scientific precision the data does not support.

### 6.3 Next Step

**Next Step** is OpFin's signature action pattern.

Every Next Step card should contain:
1. an eyebrow such as “Recommended next step”;
2. one clear action title;
3. one short explanation of why it matters;
4. one primary action;
5. optional supporting status, amount or due date.

Recommended treatment:
- Strong Indigo or Indigo surface;
- Apricot eyebrow or path accent;
- White main text;
- Periwinkle supporting text;
- Apricot primary action with Ink text when contrast permits.

Do not put competing primary actions inside a Next Step card.

### 6.4 Financial states

Use words and icons in addition to colour:
- Confirmed;
- Pending;
- Needs attention;
- Unavailable;
- Estimated.

A provider acknowledgement must not visually masquerade as completed money movement.

## 7. Iconography

Use simple outlined icons with rounded joins and consistent optical weight. Prefer familiar platform iconography for functional controls.

Custom decorative icons may borrow the Progress Path geometry, but never at the expense of recognisability.

Do not mix three icon families on one screen. The user is trying to manage money, not judge an icon-design competition.

## 8. Imagery

Photography should feel real, capable and contemporary.

Prefer:
- Ugandan and African contexts where relevant to the audience;
- natural light;
- authentic environments;
- individuals actively making, planning or discussing decisions;
- a mix of ages, genders, occupations and locations appropriate to the communication.

Avoid:
- staged cash-fanning;
- poverty-as-aesthetic imagery;
- luxury signals unsupported by the proposition;
- forced celebration over a phone;
- imagery that suggests guaranteed approval or returns;
- photographs exposing real private financial or identity information.

Crop for the human subject first and leave intentional negative space for copy.

## 9. Illustration

Use simple geometric compositions based on the Progress Path, cards, connected spaces and directional steps.

Illustration palette:
- Indigo-led foundation;
- Apricot active focus;
- Periwinkle secondary context;
- Warm Ivory/White negative space.

Illustrations may explain an unavailable-to-photograph concept but must not be labelled as a production screenshot.

## 10. Data visualisation

Default hierarchy:
- Indigo: primary series;
- Apricot: highlight/current/selected series;
- Muted/Line/Periwinkle: comparison or background series;
- Success/Warning/Danger: semantic state only.

Requirements:
- never encode meaning by colour alone;
- label axes and units;
- show UGX clearly;
- use tabular numerals;
- avoid 3-D charts;
- avoid pie/donut charts when comparison is easier as bars;
- distinguish measured values from estimates;
- state suppressed or unavailable data explicitly.

## 11. Product and interface

The product must look unmistakably OpFin without relying on a logo in every corner.

Required signals:
- Financial Compass;
- Next Step card;
- Progress Path;
- consistent financial-state wording;
- Indigo hierarchy and Apricot next-action accent;
- Inter typography;
- restrained cards and whitespace;
- trustworthy numeric formatting.

Customer Home must not visually reduce OpFin to a loan app. Borrow is a journey within the platform, not the visual definition of the platform.

## 12. Communications voice

Use plain British English.

Voice:
- clear;
- calm;
- respectful;
- practical;
- precise about uncertainty;
- human without becoming chatty about serious financial decisions.

Prefer:
- “Review your offer”
- “Payment pending confirmation”
- “We do not have this information yet”
- “Your next useful step”

Avoid:
- “Instant guaranteed cash”
- “You have won”
- “Risk-free”
- “100% approval”
- “Act now or lose access”
- unexplained financial jargon.

## 13. Documents and presentations

Controlled starter templates are in `templates/`.

Documents:
- Warm Ivory or White canvas;
- Indigo title;
- Ink body;
- Apricot/Progress Path accent;
- version/date on controlled documents;
- contact/legal information must be current, not copied from an old file.

Presentations:
- one core message per slide;
- Indigo for structure, Apricot for emphasis;
- generous margins;
- charts use the data-visualisation rules above;
- avoid tiny footnotes; disclosures remain legible.

## 14. Social and advertising

Social artwork must lead with one message, not the entire product architecture.

Preferred structure:
1. brand cue;
2. human or product truth;
3. single headline;
4. one supporting statement;
5. one action;
6. necessary disclosure.

Do not use lending urgency, countdown pressure or approval-guarantee language.

Paid advertising must distinguish eligibility from approval and activated service from platform capability.

## 15. App stores

The app icon uses the approved Indigo field with Apricot/Ivory monogram treatment.

Feature graphic:
- Warm Ivory base;
- Indigo wordmark and promise;
- Progress Path framing;
- any accent under “clearer.” stays below the glyphs;
- no unreadable disclosure text embedded in the artwork.

Screenshots must come from the exact signed release candidate. Illustration boards must never be presented as screenshots.

## 16. Partner co-branding

Use `templates/partner-cobranding.svg` as the starting structure.

Rules:
- OpFin and partner marks retain their own clear space;
- do not fuse marks;
- use a neutral divider where helpful;
- state the relationship accurately: powered by, available through, delivered with, programme partner, or another legally accurate descriptor;
- never imply a licence, underwriting role or regulatory approval that has not been verified.

## 17. Accessibility

Minimum requirements:
- WCAG AA contrast for normal text and essential controls;
- 48 px target size where practical;
- keyboard-visible focus;
- text scaling without clipping;
- TalkBack/screen-reader labels on key journeys;
- reduced-motion support;
- colour is never the only status cue;
- financial numbers wrap safely;
- disclosures remain visible at enlarged text.

## 18. Governance

The v3 release candidate is controlled by `BRAND_CHANGE_POLICY.md`.

Changes to logo geometry, colour tokens, typography, tagline, core promise, Progress Path, Financial Compass or Next Step require an explicit brand-system change proposal and evidence review.

Local teams may adapt copy and imagery for audience/context without changing the identity system or making unverified financial claims.

## 19. Release gate

Version **3.0.0** may be frozen only after the evidence listed in `README.md`, `TRADEMARK_CLEARANCE.md`, `VISUAL_QA_CHECKLIST.md` and `SCREENSHOT_CAPTURE_STANDARD.md` is complete.

Until then the controlled designation is **OpFin Brand System 3.0.0-rc.1**.
